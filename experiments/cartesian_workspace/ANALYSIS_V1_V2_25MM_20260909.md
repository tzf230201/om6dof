# OM6DOF v1 / v2 workspace comparison — 2026-09-09

## Main finding

The v2 model finds **597 more full-pose samples (+20.34% relative)** for the
tested radial gripper orientation, while finding **357 fewer position-only
samples (-2.30% relative)**. This is a trade-off in the sampled kinematic
workspace, not evidence that v2 is better for every task or physically more
accurate. Full-pose counts include near-singular solutions.

Both current source models were scanned again. The common test volume is a
500 mm-radius sphere centred on `world`, sampled every 25 mm: **33,401 unique
XYZ points**, with 41 constant planes per axis (-500 to +500 mm), 123 planes
total. Do not sum the three axis groups as independent samples.

| Found result | v1 | v2 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Position, free orientation | 15,532 (46.50%) | 15,175 (45.43%) | -1.07 percentage points |
| Full pose, tested orientation | 2,935 (8.79%) | 3,532 (10.57%) | +1.79 percentage points |

| Category | v1 | v2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Green circle: pose found | 2,738 | 3,198 |
| Blue triangle: pose found, near singular | 197 | 334 |
| Yellow square: position found, orientation unresolved | 12,597 | 11,643 |
| Purple diamond: only colliding candidates found | 90 | 358 |
| Red X: position unresolved | 17,779 | 17,868 |

Near-singular witnesses increase from 6.71% to 9.46% of found poses. The pose
coverage gain therefore comes with more selected configurations that are poorly
conditioned; neither classification proves that another better solution is absent.

## Same-coordinate overlap

Results are joined by exact XYZ, not CSV row order.

| Metric | Both found | v1 only | v2 only | Neither found |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Position | 14,391 | 1,141 | 784 | 17,085 |
| Full pose | 2,829 | 106 | 703 | 29,763 |

For example, v2 finds a full pose at 703 points where v1 did not; it fails to
find one at 106 points where v1 did. The net gain of 597 does not mean every
previously successful sample is retained. "Only" here describes this finite
search, not a proof of geometric impossibility for the other model.

Selected constant-coordinate planes illustrate the spatial trade-off:

## What changed and what was held constant

- v1 `joint3` origin: `[0, 0, 124] mm`, zero origin rotation.
- v2 `joint3` origin: `[24, 0, 128] mm`, +90 degrees about Y.
- Joint limits are identical between the two snapshots used in this run.
- Conservative chain-length bounds: v1 491.308652 mm, v2 497.539217 mm.
- Shared scan radius: 500 mm, large enough to cover both bounds.
- Target: `end_effector_link` relative to `world`.
- Orientation: radial; horizontal outward gripper, yaw = atan2(Y, X).
- 2,000 seed samples; four IK seeds; 100 iterations; RNG seed 42.
- Joint margin: 0.02 rad; capsule collision radius: 25 mm.
- Position tolerance: 1 mm; orientation tolerance: 0.5 degrees.
- Jacobian length scale: 300 mm; near-singular threshold: rcond < 0.01.

Identical random-seed settings and budgets do not imply identical numerical
seed banks: FK positions and nearest-seed selection depend on the model. The
scanner source and executable are the same in both runs.

The earlier public 25 mm demo used a 491.308652 mm radius and 31,799 points.
The new v1 run reproduces every field of all 31,799 common historical rows;
the additional 1,602 outer samples are unresolved. Its percentage is lower
because the denominator is larger. Compare v1 and v2 in this matched report,
not percentages from different-radius demonstrations.

## Limits of the mechanical interpretation

This scanner uses the URDF serial chain and simplified chain-capsule endpoint
collision tests. **It does not read visual/collision triangle meshes or inertial
mass/CoM values.** The D435 camera/bracket geometry, new link3 mesh fit, payload
capacity and gravity dynamics are not assessed. Only the chain transforms and
joint limits influence this kinematic comparison.

Finite-grid, finite-budget IK failure is not proof of unreachable space. Each
sample tests one orientation, not all possible rotations. Collision is checked
at the candidate configuration, not along a trajectory, and no table/floor or
external obstacles are modelled. A found sample is not permission to command
the physical robot there. The sphere includes negative Z.

The practical conclusion is that the v2 kinematics offer more found solutions
for this particular outward-horizontal gripper task, with some position-coverage
loss and increased near-singular/colliding-candidate counts. Confirm important
task poses with a larger IK budget and full mesh/path collision checking before
using them on hardware.
